# Design basis and limits

Scott E. Sampson, *Essentials of Service Design and Innovation*, fourth edition, Chapters 1–6 supplied by the user, pp. 36–37, distinguishes co-production of value potential from subsequent value realization. Pages 47–48 discuss customer control, provider responsibility, customization and efficiency. The material does not establish that every user contribution improves an experience.

Apply this distinction by asking users for decisions that determine useful outcomes while letting the agent perform specialized technical work. Defining an expected quote-form outcome is shared preparation; a correctly diagnosed failure or verified repair makes that preparation useful. This application and the first-result conversation design are implementation choices, not experimental findings from the book.

Do not redistribute the supplied PDF. Do not assert that co-creation necessarily produces delight, attachment, confidence, retention or willingness to pay. Do not conflate participation with valuable participation.
